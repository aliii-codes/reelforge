# title: How a laptop runs a 7B AI model (voice-over demo)
# topic: How quantization lets small AI models run locally
# caption: Why can a laptop run a 7B AI model? Quantization: storing each weight with fewer bits. Big memory savings, small quality cost, until you squeeze too hard.
# hashtags: #AI #LLM #Quantization #TechExplained #MachineLearning
# say: CPU = C P U

[hook]
A seven-billion-parameter AI model can run on an ordinary laptop CPU. No data center required.
> headline: A 7B AI model on a laptop | kicker: No data center
> expression: surprised

[explain]
Here's how it actually works. A model is a giant list of numbers called parameters, or weights. To use it, your computer has to hold those numbers in memory.
> scene: diagram | headline: What running a model means | items: A giant list of numbers ; Called weights ; Held in memory
> icon: layers

[number]
At sixteen-bit precision, every parameter takes two bytes. So seven billion parameters need about fourteen gigabytes, just for the weights.
> scene: stat | value: 14 | suffix: GB | label: 7B weights at 16-bit | headline: Memory needed

[beat]
The trick is quantization. You store each weight with fewer bits, like four instead of sixteen. Now the same model needs roughly three and a half gigabytes.
> scene: demo | headline: Quantization in practice | lines: $ quantize model --bits 4 ; # 16-bit weights -> 4-bit ; size: 14 GB -> 3.5 GB
> expression: happy

[analogy]
Think of it like saving a huge photo as a smaller JPEG. You lose a little detail, but it still looks right.
> scene: comparison | left: Huge, detailed photo | right: Small JPEG, still looks right | headline: Quantization is compression

[twist]
But here's the catch. Squeeze too hard, down to one or two bits, and quality starts to drop. Those extreme settings need careful calibration just to stay accurate.
> headline: Too few bits hurts quality | kicker: BUT…

[payoff]
So small local models aren't magic. They're clever compression.
> headline: Not magic. Clever compression. | icon: bulb

[cta]
Send this to a friend who thinks AI needs a supercomputer.
> headline: Local AI is just smart math | kicker: Send this to a friend

[claim] Quantization lowers memory requirements by storing the weights in a lower precision. | https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/quantization/overview
[claim] At 4 bits per weight, seven billion parameters need roughly 3.5 GB (7 billion x 0.5 bytes). | https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/llm_tutorial_optimization
[claim] Model weights can be quantized to 8-bit or 4-bit without a significant loss in performance. | https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/llm_tutorial_optimization
