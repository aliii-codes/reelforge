# title: How AI models learn from data
# topic: How neural networks are trained: data, guesses, and tiny weight updates
# caption: AI models aren't programmed with answers. They guess, measure how wrong they were, and nudge billions of weights. Repeat. That's training.
# hashtags: #AI #MachineLearning #NeuralNetworks #TechExplained #LearnAI
# say: GPT-3 = G P T three

[hook]
An AI model starts out knowing absolutely nothing. So how does it end up writing code and answering questions?
> headline: It starts out knowing nothing | kicker: How does AI learn?
> expression: surprised

[explain]
A model is a neural network: layers of simple math units, connected by billions of numbers called weights.
> scene: diagram | headline: A neural network | items: Input ; Layer 1 ; Layer 2 ; Output

[explain]
First, it needs data. Training feeds it huge amounts of text, images, and examples from the internet.
> step: data

[explain]
Second, it guesses. For each example, the model predicts the answer, like the next word in a sentence.
> step: guess | scene: diagram | headline: Predict the next word | items: The cat ; sat on the ; mat

[explain]
Third, it adjusts. It measures how wrong the guess was, then nudges every weight a tiny bit to be less wrong.
> step: adjust

[number]
GPT-3 has one hundred seventy-five billion weights, and every single one was tuned this way.
> scene: stat | value: 175 | suffix: B | label: weights in GPT-3 | headline: Tuned one nudge at a time

[analogy]
It's like learning darts blindfolded, with a friend shouting "a bit to the left." Every throw lands a little closer.
> left: Darts: throw, miss, aim again | right: AI: guess, measure error, nudge weights

[twist]
But here's the catch. It only learns the patterns in its data. Feed it biased or wrong examples, and it learns those too.
> headline: It learns your data's mistakes too | kicker: BUT…

[payoff]
So an AI model doesn't memorize a textbook. It learns patterns, one tiny nudge at a time.
